How It Actually Works Lab

Move the sliders. Watch the physics.

Each Lab is the 3D model from one of our Shorts, live: change the load, the size or the turns and see what the part does, with every number worked out.

Buckling: 2× the length, a quarter of the load

Two pinned struts of the same 40 × 10 mm steel bar, 500 and 1000 mm long: the long one buckles at 6.9 kN, the short one at 27.6 kN, though crushing the steel would take 110 kN. Bows are drawn 6× larger than they are, and stop where the steel starts to yield.

Capstan: three turns hold 43× what one turn holds

Rope round a steel bollard: friction multiplies the hand's pull by the same factor every turn, e^(2πμ) = 6.6 at μ = 0.3. So turns multiply, they don't add: T₂ = T₁·e^(μθ).

Gear ratio: 3× the torque, not 3× the work

A 16-tooth gear drives one with 48 teeth. The big gear gives 3 times the torque, but it turns only a third as far, so the work out equals the work in.

Torsion: the same steel as a tube is 3.2× stiffer

A solid Ø40 mm steel bar and a Ø58/42 mm tube hold the same 1257 mm² of steel; twisted by 1000 N·m over 1 m, the bar turns 2.85° and the tube only 0.89°. Shear stress grows with the radius (τ = T·r / J), so steel far from the axis does the work.

Beam bending: on edge, 4× stiffer than flat

The same 50 × 100 mm steel bar across a 2 m span, 10 kN at mid-span: flat it sags 8 mm, on edge only 2 mm - 4 times stiffer, not 2, because depth counts cubed (I = b h³/12). Sags are drawn 15× larger than they are; the colours show the bending stress (blue squeezed, red stretched), full at yield (250 MPa).
